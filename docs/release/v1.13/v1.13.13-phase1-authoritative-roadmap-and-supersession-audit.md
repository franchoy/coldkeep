project intent require before v1.x may close? It freezes the roadmap hierarchy

## 3. Chronological roadmap evolution

1. The v1.0 baseline established correctness-first storage guarantees:
   deterministic restore, integrity, recovery, crash safety, and GC safety.
2. The May 2026 frozen v1.10 revised roadmap inserted reliability stabilization
   before architectural extraction, then assigned Engine introduction to
   v1.11, logic migration to v1.12, and stable headless Engine contracts to
   v1.13.
3. The v1.11 scope made the Engine facade behavior-preserving, retained
   PostgreSQL compatibility, described SQLite-first portability as conditional
   v1.12/v1.13 direction, and put daemon/UI/network product work after v1.
4. The v1.12 train introduced Engine/Catalog ownership but explicitly retained
   deferred operations for v1.13 rather than claiming complete migration.
5. The v1.13 release train converted those broad goals into staged contract,
   invariant, backend, portability, restore/remove, and thin-CLI work.
6. v1.13.7 froze a future repository-local SQLite portability target while
   explicitly proving that normal runtime still used PostgreSQL and that no
   default-backend switch occurred.
7. v1.13.9 and v1.13.10 temporarily concluded that remaining architecture and
   backend work could be deferred and that v1.x could close. Their v2 local
   product and v3 beyond-local split remained useful, but the closure decision
   used a narrower completion definition.
8. The post-v1.13.10 roadmap-to-code audit restored v1.13.11–v1.13.13 because
   backend proof, same-host coordination, and Engine/Catalog completion were
   still mandatory before v2.
9. v1.13.11 closed the bounded backend, coordination, cross-platform, and
   correctness-hardening gate while keeping SQLite-default productization in
   v2 and cross-host/network-filesystem coordination out of the v1 claim.
10. v1.13.12 completed the mandatory Engine/Catalog architecture and thin-CLI
    work. Its canonical release is complete and operationally closed.
11. v1.13.13 Phase 0 activated the final audit in
    `AUDIT_PLUS_REMEDIATION` mode after the independent audit found
    `RESTORE_INSTALL_CONTRACT` still unresolved.

## 4. Supersession rules

Current intent is resolved by these rules, in order:

1. Explicit current-status and supersession statements outrank dates and older
   roadmap promises.
2. A later release decision may supersede scope or timing, but never rewrites
   the factual evidence of a completed release.
3. Completed-release evidence proves what shipped; it does not independently
   define current future scope.
4. A deferral is valid only when a later roadmap explicitly owns it and retains
   the underlying v1 correctness and compatibility guarantees.
5. A historical requirement cannot become a current blocker unless current
   authority reaffirms it.
6. A specific current decision controls over a broad older aspiration.
7. Git timestamps assist chronology but never determine authority alone.

## 6. v1.x normative requirements

Before v1.x closes, current authority requires:

- the v1 correctness, restore, GC, verification, recovery/repair, format,
  bounds, decompression, numeric, mutation, and concurrency guarantees;
- a complete reusable headless Engine with neutral requests/results, typed
  errors, and no CLI-only correctness sequencing;
- Catalog ownership and production adoption for graph, placement, restore-plan,
  and GC-plan decisions with a strict Catalog/storage/Engine responsibility
  split;
- an enforceably thin CLI that retains human, JSON, and exit compatibility;
- honest SQLite/PostgreSQL contract evidence with backend-specific differences
  explicit;
- same-host/local-filesystem repository coordination within the documented
  platform and PostgreSQL advisory-session bounds;
- Linux/macOS/Windows evidence for the claimed native/path/restore contracts;
- complete local, hosted, adversarial, security, and release-lifecycle gates;
  and
- zero genuine blockers at the Phase 13 formal closure decision.

This is a requirements freeze, not an implementation PASS claim.

## 8. v2.x productization boundary

v2.x owns local-first productization without being authorized by this phase:

- `coldkeepd` and one local mutation owner;
- a local mutation queue and daemon-owned maintenance barriers;
- SQLite as the default local repository catalog;
- repository-local `.coldkeep/catalog.sqlite` lifecycle;
- clean-close/checkpoint, move/copy, reopen, and validate portability workflows;
- CLI operation as a daemon-backed thin client;
- local API and optional local UI;
- local scheduling and automation;
- catalog export/import/backup; and
- local offline verification/recovery product workflows.

PostgreSQL compatibility is retained through v2. The v2 product layer may not
weaken v1 correctness guarantees.

## 9. v3.x network-expansion boundary

v3.x or later owns:

- a network-exposed service and LAN/remote clients;
- NAS and multiple storage-root productization;
- NFS, SMB, and arbitrary network-filesystem semantics;
- centralized PostgreSQL product/server mode;
- multi-user identity and authorization;
- cloud/object storage;
- replication and synchronization; and
- cross-machine coordination and distributed operation.

These items are not v1 blockers and do not enter v2 without an explicit future
roadmap revision.

## 12. Resolution for every contradiction

- The v1.13.11 post-release correction and current train supersede the former
  v1.13.10 closure conclusion without invalidating the release.
- Active Phase 0 scope supersedes audit-only language.
- SQLite backend obligations remain in v1; SQLite-default portable product
  behavior is owned by v2.
- v1.13.12 evidence supersedes old Engine/Catalog and repair/recovery
  deferrals. Phase 2 must independently audit the current end-state.
- The two meanings of thin CLI are recorded as distinct architectures.
- Same-host/local-filesystem coordination is the bounded v1 guarantee;
  cross-host and network-filesystem guarantees are v3.
- PostgreSQL compatibility remains mandatory; only centralized server product
  mode is v3.
- The current v2/v3 boundary supersedes older broad "later v2.x" wording for
  network/NAS/cloud work.

No unresolved authority contradiction remains.
